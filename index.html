Lagos Under Water— mapping flood exposure across extreme rainfall events

Three 7-day rainfall events from 2015–2024 that ranked in the 95th percentile for Lagos were selected from CHIRPS satellite observations. For each, an Event-based Flood Exposure Index (EFEI) combines elevation, water proximity and rainfall intensity to show who is at risk when the storm hits.

Methodology & Rainfall Threshold

EFEI — Event-based Flood Exposure Index

EFEI = 0.30 × Elevation_risk + 0.30 × Water_proximity_risk + 0.40 × Rainfall_risk

All three components are normalised to 0–1 before combination. Elevation risk is highest in low-lying land (≤ 30 m); water-proximity risk is highest within ~1.5 km of permanent water; rainfall risk comes from the 7-day accumulated rainfall over each storm event.

What counts as "extreme" rainfall?

Extreme rainfall events are defined using both meteorological standards and regional hydrological evidence:

  • Absolute threshold: > 50 mm/day — the WMO classification for heavy-to-extreme precipitation in tropical environments, widely used in West-African flood studies.
  • Statistical threshold: the top 5 % (95th percentile) of 7-day accumulated rainfall from the CHIRPS daily product, 2015 – 2024. For Lagos this works out to 91.5 mm over 7 days.

The combined approach keeps the study consistent with international standards while capturing locally relevant extremes. From all candidate events above the threshold, an algorithm picks the three heaviest episodes that are at least 7 days apart — the events shown on this map.

Data Sources

  • Rainfall: CHIRPS Daily (UCSB-CHG) — 0.05° grid, 2015 – 2024.
  • Elevation: NASADEM HGT (NASA) — ~30 m spatial resolution.
  • Surface water: JRC Global Surface Water v1.4 — permanent-water mask at 30 m.
  • Processing: Google Earth Engine. Output: 30 m classified GeoTIFFs.

Reading the Classes

  • Class 1 — Low  EFEI ≤ 0.33 · higher elevation, far from water, minor rainfall impact.
  • Class 2 — Medium  0.33 < EFEI ≤ 0.66 · transitional zones with mixed risk drivers.
  • Class 3 — High  EFEI > 0.66 · low-lying coastal and lagoon-adjacent land hit hard by the storm.
